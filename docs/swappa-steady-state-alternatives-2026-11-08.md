# Swappa without the steady state: learning-consistent finite-sample layers

Date: 8 November 2026. Audience: Lorenzo and Niccolò (and their agents). Status: assessment with new computations; no manuscript, response, or branch change; nothing pushed or sent. Companion to `docs/swappa-defensible-results-assessment-2026-11-08.md`. Numbers are in `docs/evidence/swappa_learning_layer_20261108.json`; the producing script is `python/swappa_rescue/swappa_learning_layer_20261108.py`, with the decision-granularity diagnostics in `output/swappa_core_20261108/decision_granularity.csv`.

## 1. The objection, stated precisely

The paper's asymptotic result says that no-regret play converges to the BCCE set. Its finite-sample layer (Theorem CR and the learning-design families) says how far realized play can be from that set after N rounds, through a regret envelope. The revival's Assumption A2 replaces the envelope by a statement about the process: the window is a stationary stretch of play whose one-period law already lies in the limit set. That is an equilibrium-in-distribution assumption. It makes the application silent about learning, it is testable only through subwindow agreement, and it concedes the point the paper is built on, that one need not assume play has converged. The question is what restriction on the window can be justified from the paper's own logic and from what the data show about these sellers as learners.

## 2. What the data show

**Decision granularity.** The sellers price per SKU-day, not per day and not per listing. For GadgetPickup, 97 percent of SKU-day cells carry one price, there are about 62 price cells on a day for 128 listings, price changes occur on 82 of 164 days and touch 13 SKUs on a typical change day, and there is no day on which the seller moves its whole portfolio proportionally. The same holds for every seller with at least 300 sales (13 to 100 cells a day; seller-wide proportional moves on zero to seven days). So the learner's round is a SKU-day decision: about 10,000 rounds in the window for GadgetPickup, and its action is the markup over that SKU's market, not a daily level.

**Experience.** Rating counts displayed next to the seller's name give presample Swappa sales (branch `longevity.py`): GadgetPickup 7,939 sales, about 102,000 listing-days or 49,000 SKU-day rounds before the window; We Buy Mobile 171,000 rounds; AMS 38,000; Upgrade Solution 1,300 (half a year); Certified Cell 800 (two months). Experience varies by two orders of magnitude across the thirteen sellers.

**Information.** What we scraped is what a seller sees: every rival listing and every sale on the platform, daily. A professional seller can evaluate demand at any markup from the market, not only from its own sales. This is the full-feedback assumption of the paper's original application, and it matters: with realized own sales as the only feedback, every envelope is vacuous at every clock (below), the sparse-outcome mechanism the V05 lab exhibited.

**Behavior across experience.** The two youngest sellers are the erratic ones (monthly profit-maximizing level deviations of 3.9 and 4.7 percent on average for Certified Cell and Wireless Source, against 0.8 for GadgetPickup), but across all thirteen the relation between experience and dispersion is flat (Spearman about zero; the two oldest small sellers are themselves dispersed, and their experience figures are extrapolations). The learning signature is visible at the young end only.

**Comparators and information.** Holding each seller's markup fixed at a uniform value across all its SKU-days is rejected at fitted demand for GadgetPickup, TD'S, and PDX; fixed markups per phone generation or per model are rejected for several more. The level-shift comparator, which keeps everything the seller does except the scale, is never rejected. The reading is not that sellers leave money on the table but that they condition on SKU-day information (condition details, specific competitors, inventory) that the demand model does not have. A comparator that preserves the seller's information is the right one when the econometrician's model is coarser than the seller's; the external fixed-rule comparators measure model coarseness as if it were regret.

## 3. Three layers that replace A2, and what they deliver

All three keep the paper's own assumption of a stationary environment (i.i.d. states) and assume nothing about the stationarity of play.

**E(H): experience-indexed relaxed obedience.** A certified full-information learner (the paper's family F) started H rounds before the window has, in a stationary environment, an expected per-round regret during the window of at most eps(H) = 2 K_round sqrt(ln d / H), with K_round the payoff range over the d = 9 actions in that round. Summing over a day's rounds gives a daily allowance, and the window's population inequalities become the paper's relaxed-obedience set Q_r(eps(H)) with sampling error on top. Nothing is assumed about convergence: eps(H) is positive, shrinks with experience, and A2 is its H to infinity limit.

**AW: any-window pathwise envelope.** A strongly adaptive full-information learner has regret on every interval bounded by sqrt(sum_rounds K_round^2) times [sqrt(2 ln(d T_life)) + sqrt(2 ln(d / alpha))] (the fixed-share and FLH constructions). No H, no stationarity of anything, pathwise, so no sampling term. It is the revival's tier T2 moved from the daily clock, where it was vacuous, to the SKU-day clock the data show.

**Bandit on realized own sales.** Same as E(H) with the realized-profit range in cash units and the sqrt(d ln d / H) rate.

## 4. What this asks of the method

1. An instantaneous regret bound for family F in a stationary environment (expected per-round regret at round t at most c K sqrt(ln d / t)), or the branch's no-deterioration lemma as the bridge from the cumulative bound. E(H) rests on it; the constant 2 above is the branch's convention.
2. The batched decision structure: about 62 simultaneous SKU-day decisions a day, each with its own payoff vector, one learner across cells (the level-shift and uniform-markup versions) or one per context (the contextual versions, the paper's own notion). Which clock the regret bound runs on is a modeling statement the data support, not a free choice.
3. Any-window constants for family F (fixed share or FLH), so that AW is certified rather than borrowed.
4. The deviation class. Proportional level shifts are translations in log markup; they have no fixed point, so the Gordon, Greenwald, and Marks construction does not apply, and the natural reading is first-order stationarity (no gain from local rescalings), which gradient methods on a markup satisfy and Appendix C's internal regret implies. The paper can either state A3 that way or adopt external regret on the SKU-day markup (uniform or contextual) and treat its rejections as informative about the model.
5. Sampling theory. Under E(H) the inequality is on expectations and the window average has a martingale-difference plus i.i.d.-state structure, so HAC inference is appropriate; under AW the bound is pathwise and only the demand estimate's uncertainty remains (not added here).

## 5. How the H unobserved rounds enter the paper's framework, exactly

Every regret object in the paper is a prefix object: Definition 1 sums deviation gains from period 1 of the online-learning environment, Theorem 1 concerns the empirical distribution of that whole history, and Theorem CR with the family F and B envelopes concerns "the actual initialized run". The window is rounds H+1 to H+T of a learner that started at round 1. One consequence per theorem.

1. **Theorem 1 (asymptotics).** The window's empirical distribution is the prefix distribution through H+T minus the one through H, reweighted. With H fixed and T growing the two share their cluster points, so Theorem 1 and the identified set carry over. With H large relative to T they do not: ANR is a budget constraint on each deviation's running total of gains, the window's gain is the total at H+T minus the total at H, and the subtracted total can be of order H times a per-round margin, since a good learner beats a bad deviation every round of its history. Divided by T this is a bound of order H/T, not zero: a learner with a long history has banked slack against every deviation and ANR lets it spend that slack in a short window. H/T is 30 and 24 for the two oldest sellers, about 5 for GadgetPickup and AMS, below one only for Certified Cell. A2 asserted what ANR does not deliver, that the window is representative of the limit.
2. **Theorem CR (pathwise finite sample).** The draft already states the point (Section 4: "a continuing learner requires a bound that allows its initial state"; appendix on the observation window: "An arbitrary later subwindow of a continuing learner does not inherit a prefix bound"). The adversarial envelopes hold from a start of bounded complexity, ln(1/p0(a)) at most C; after H rounds in a stationary market ln(1/p_H(a)) for a bad action is of order step size times H times gap, a high-complexity start to which no prefix bound applies. The two layers are the two ways of supplying a bound that allows the initial state.
3. **E(H): change the regret object, keep the clock running.** Expected per-round regret in a stationary stochastic environment is the object the application section already uses ("stochastic i.i.d. payoff environment with full feedback", an algorithm optimizing the rate of expected regret, Proposition 2 via Faure et al. 2015, Section 1.2). In that setting per-round pseudo-regret (best expected payoff in the seller's context minus the expected payoff of its action) is nonnegative, so every deviation's expected window gain is at most the window's pseudo-regret, which is at most the whole run's. Two cases. (a) A horizon-free cumulative bound, such as Proposition 2's gap-dependent constant, passes to the window unchanged with N the window length: Proposition 2 is already valid for a continuing learner, and H is irrelevant; its weakness is the gap dependence the draft's own note flags (levels one percent apart have gaps near zero). (b) With the gap-free square-root bound the window inherits only the bound at H+T divided by T, worse than a restart once H exceeds T, so the per-round rate is needed: pseudo-regret at round s at most c K sqrt(ln d / s) for a decreasing-step learner in a stationary environment (weight concentration, or the no-deterioration lemma: a non-increasing per-round regret is bounded by its running average, which the cumulative bound controls). The window allowance is the average of this over s = H+1, ..., H+T, in closed form 2 c K sqrt(ln d) (sqrt(H+T) - sqrt(H)) / T. H enters exactly there, as the lower limit of the sum. H = 0 gives 2 c K sqrt(ln d / T), the restart calibration of the paper's family F bound; H to infinity gives zero, steady state; H from the ratings gives E(H). The statistical object changes with the regret object: the inequality is on expectations, the window average of model-implied gains estimates it, i.i.d. states with an adapted learner give a martingale-difference structure, HAC applies; the regret term is the allowance, the sampling term the critical value times the standard error, the paper's own separation of the regret and consistency components with composition conditioned on observed states. Since one history is observed, the per-round bound must hold conditional on the learner's realized state (a high-probability bound with a ln(1/delta) term), which is the form the constant should be certified in.
4. **AW: keep the pathwise object, change the algorithm class.** Strongly adaptive learners (Hazan and Seshadhri's adaptive regret; Daniely, Gonen, and Shalev-Shwartz; fixed share as the simplest member) have realized regret on every interval of length tau at most a constant times K sqrt(tau ln(d T_life)), whatever the state at the interval's start: literally a bound that allows the initial state. H enters only through ln(T_life) = ln(H+T), the union over interval starts. Theorem CR applies to the window with this envelope through the direct-tail calibration, no stationarity and no sampling term.
5. **Not nested.** A concentrating learner has vanishing per-round regret in a stationary world and no interval guarantee in a changing one; an adaptive learner keeps weight on alternatives, so its per-round regret does not vanish with H while its interval guarantee holds in any world. The window cannot separate them; the flat experience-dispersion relation across sellers leans toward the adaptive picture or heterogeneous environments, the monthly stability of the top sellers fits either. Both are reported.
6. **Deviation class and the seller's information.** Level shifts are swaps, hence directly in Appendix C's contextual internal regret; under external regret they are implied whenever the seller's price is a function of its own information, since a swap of a measurable action is a contextual fixed-action deviation at the seller's finer partition, which the paper allows. The pooled 1/sqrt(H) assumes one rule across cells; separate learning per SKU makes the pooled allowance a lower bound on the true one by Jensen's inequality, by up to the square root of the number of cells.
7. **For the manuscript.** Proposition 2 survives with N the window and the horizon-free constant replaced by the H-indexed per-round envelope; the hook is the Section 4 sentence. Niccolò certifies one lemma (conditional per-round pseudo-regret rate of family F in an i.i.d. payoff environment, with constant) and adds one row to the appendix table (interval envelope of a strongly adaptive family). The measured H counts rounds on the platform, a lower bound on rounds on the same rule only absent restarts, which is what the monthly stability check guards.

## 6. Recommendation

Keep A2 only as the H to infinity benchmark. State the maintained finite-sample assumption as a stationary environment plus a certified full-information learner on the SKU-day clock with experience bounded below by the rating counts, report the relaxed-obedience sets with that allowance as the headline (GadgetPickup [8, 17]), the steady-state limit alongside ([10, 15]), and the any-window bound as the assumption-light statement ([4, 20]). Present the thirteen sellers ordered by experience so the paper can say what the theory predicts: precision grows with the learner's experience, young sellers are set-identified only loosely, and a seller with two months on the platform tells us nothing yet. Keep the level-shift comparator as the maintained restriction because it preserves the seller's information; report the uniform and contextual fixed-markup comparators as stronger alternatives whose rejections are informative about the demand model. State plainly that the seller's ability to evaluate demand from the market, not any convergence of its play, is what makes the application informative, and that realized-sales feedback alone would make it vacuous.
