## Primitive restrictions and payoff bound

Let q(p,z) be a unit's daily sale probability, c its persistent dollar cost, and m=p-c its markup. Maintain the following restrictions on the entire structural state support, not merely on the observed sample:

1. The learner excludes below-cost prices (the option not to offer yields zero).
2. q(c,z) <= qbar < 1.
3. The price derivative of logit q(p,z) is at most -k0 < 0 for every p>=c, including either side of the rival-minimum kink.

Integrating the third restriction gives q(c+m,z) <= expit(logit(qbar)-k0*m). Maximizing m times this upper envelope over m>=0 gives the uniform profit bound

    K(qbar,k0) = W(qbar/[e(1-qbar)])/k0,

where W is the principal Lambert W function. All permitted expected profits therefore belong to [0,K]. The result does not require excluding high prices or asserting that the observed maximum is a support bound. It also does not assume an iid sequence of costs, competitive states, or sale probabilities. The full-information feedback assumption still applies to the exact expected structural payoff.

For Upgrade Solution, the pilot specifies reference-price support [100,400] dollars and k0=.03 per dollar. Its fitted quality-demand primitive satisfies this derivative restriction throughout that reference range, on both sides of the price kink. This is a parameter/support restriction of the maintained model, not a claim that the estimated coefficient has no uncertainty. At qbar=.75, .90 and .99 the corresponding K values are 20.118, 36.700 and 87.622 dollars per listing-day. The qbar=.50 version is incompatible with persistent costs and no-below-cost posting for eight of the 897 analyzed listings; it is reported as rejected, not trimmed into feasibility.

The action menu contains 501 integer residual-price policies, eleven dollar-markup policies, and no offer. Each reference-price policy clips its proposed price from below at the seller's known cost. All observed integer posted prices can be generated by this menu when the candidate cost satisfies no-below-cost posting. The inverse program tests eleven reference-price policies, all eleven markup policies, and no offer; using a subset yields outer restrictions. The ten-rate and eight-rate versions are distinct algorithms; this implementation uses eight predeclared dyadic rates per policy.

## Continuous-cost outer relaxation

For each listing l, no-below-cost posting gives c_l <= min_t p_lt. Monotonic demand and q(c_l,z_lt)<=qbar give a lower cost bound at each observed state; take the maximum across the listing's life and zero. Incompatible listings reject that specification. There is no assumed cost distribution or independence restriction on latent costs.

Partition each listing's feasible cost interval into G cells. A variable x_lg represents mass assigned to cell g, with sum_g x_lg=1. The same assignment is used on every observed day of the listing. This remains an outer relaxation of a deterministic assignment of one cost to each listing.

Let c0 be a cell midpoint and h its half-width. For a reference policy, the expert payoff is q(p_a)(p_a-c)_+, which is piecewise affine in c. For a markup policy m, the expert payoff is m q(c+m). The implementation bounds the absolute derivative of each excess payoff r(c) throughout the cell, including all clipping and rival-price kinks. Thus r(c)>=r(c0)-hL and r(c)^2 <= (|r(c0)|+hL)^2. Sum these lower and upper bounds over listing days to obtain R_lower and V_upper. For every predetermined learning rate eta, impose

    sum_lg x_lg [R_lower_lg - eta V_upper_lg]
        <= D [log(A J) + H + log(D/alpha_R)] / eta.

The code divides both sides by the number of listing-days. D is the number of separate interleaved learners, not an effective iid sample size. It reports D=1,10,100 and H=0 or 5 log(A). Alpha_R=.005 leaves room for first-stage uncertainty and selection among the five initially screened sellers, but that first-stage component has not yet been implemented.

For the lower cost objective, weight cell lower endpoints; for the upper objective, weight cell upper endpoints. Every true continuous cost has a cell witness satisfying these inequalities on the stated regret event. The output is consequently a continuous-cost outer relaxation, rather than a discrete-grid cost model. Refinement from 30 to 60 cells checks numerical sensitivity.

## Initial numerical result and interpretation

With G=30, qbar=.75, D=1, H=0 and the fitted demand primitive, the sold-listing mean cost outer range is [100.99,134.28] dollars. The corresponding gross posted-price markup range is [6.29,39.59], at mean observed transaction price 140.57. The primitive restrictions alone give costs [90.74,140.49], so the regret restrictions add information. Allowing H=5 log(A) gives costs [98.09,138.47] and gross markups [2.10,42.48].

At qbar=.90, D=1 and H=0, costs are [91.72,137.58] and gross markups [3.00,48.85], compared with prior-only costs [67.80,140.49]. With H=5 log(A), costs are [85.65,140.49], so the positive markup floor is essentially the participation restriction. D=10 generally removes meaningful positive markup lower bounds; D=100 is close to prior-only information. These are conditional diagnostics, not final confidence intervals.

## Remaining economic and inferential gates

- First-stage uncertainty, causal price effects, timing of sales, and the precise feedback/decision clock remain unresolved. The economic payoff envelope fixes the data-dependent-range problem; it does not fix these other issues.
- The current demand model treats listing profits as separable conditional on own inventory count. This is consequential: Upgrade Solution has about 24 own listings in a matched product market on the average listing-day, with own share about 45 percent. Substitution among its own listings can change the seller's objective. `multiproduct_demand.py` is testing an own-peer-price term. A credible extension must account for total own-inventory profit, or explicitly restrict the estimand to single-own-listing states and justify contextual learning there. Do not promote the numerical candidate before resolving this issue.
- The scalar dollar cost is a current inventory opportunity value in a general dynamic inventory model. An interpretation as an all-in marginal replacement cost requires a replenishment model. With immediate replacement of a sold unit at current procurement cost and a stable continuation value, the one-period payoff has the displayed q(p)(p-c) form. The data show substantial entry/replenishment but do not prove this exact model. Original acquisition cost is generally sunk.
- All prices and costs here are gross-price equivalents. Historical seller/buyer/payment fees, shipping, returns, and the mapping to net proceeds have not been settled.
- The currently fitted logistic primitive satisfies the support slope restriction; other demand specifications and a demand confidence set must also be intersected with the maintained parameter restriction. A model-specific bound cannot be carried unchanged to a different fitted model that violates its assumptions.
