## 1. The answer in one paragraph

The defensible result is the steady-state revealed-preference bound on markups: for the largest Swappa dealers, no uniform rescaling of the day's price level raises expected profit on average, so the seller's cost-to-value ratio is set-identified, and the Lerner index of sold phones is in the low teens, with the whole range of fitted demand specifications spanning 7 to 17 percent for GadgetPickup and similar ranges for AMS Traders, Upgrade Solution, and We Buy Mobile. This result survives every inference repair tried today (joint max-t, demand-moment cross-covariance, a 500-replication block bootstrap that refits demand), survives integer prices, the reference-price reconstruction, richer rival controls, product-by-week effects, and a repricing-freshness control, and holds under the paper's own regret notion applied to a level action as well as under the branch's level-shift comparator. Its one fragile input is the demand response, exactly as in any markup study; the flexible-shape specification lowers the index by 3 to 4 points and a 25 percent attenuation raises it by about 4. The counterfactuals (fee incidence, recommending platform) are illustrations that inherit that fragility through demand curvature and should be presented as such, not as headline numbers.

For these four sellers every test is valid or conservative, and the three sets agree within half a point for GadgetPickup. The picture is different for thin sellers: Wireless Source (389 sales) rejects 28 percent of the time under Bonferroni and 32 percent under max-t, and its nominal 95 percent Bonferroni set covers the fitted midpoint in only 73 percent of replications; The Cellular Resource and Gilly's reject 7 to 12 percent. Conclusion: the asymptotic inference is reliable for the four sellers above and should not be quoted at nominal level for the thin sellers without the bootstrap. The percentile-t bootstrap is the inference of record I would propose.

Three repairs asked for in the 6 November memo are closed by these computations.

- M3, coverage. The demand-moment cross-covariance, computed from the date-aggregated influence functions of the logit with the same Bartlett kernel, is small and of either sign: between minus 7 and plus 2 percent of the moment variance for GadgetPickup, up to plus 31 percent for We Buy Mobile's price cuts. Adding it moves the max-t set by at most 0.3 points for either seller. The full block bootstrap with demand refit is the controlled check the memo wanted; the analytic standard errors are within 10 percent of the bootstrap dispersion in the fitted set for GadgetPickup (ratios 0.88 to 0.99 across the eight shifts) and conservative for most sellers. The two-way clustered standard error of the above-rival-minimum slope of the logit (the sum of the two collinear price terms) is 0.347 against a bootstrap standard deviation of 0.367.
- M6, feasible prices. Integer-rounded deviations with owned-peer means recomputed from rounded prices give the same Bonferroni set as continuous scaling for GadgetPickup and sets within one grid step (0.25 points) for every other seller.
- M5, reference construction. The same three designs fitted on the existing Stata-derived reference panel give GadgetPickup [11.0, 16.7], [10.4, 16.2], and [7.7, 11.8] (Bonferroni; baseline, richer rivals, flexible) against [10.0, 15.4], [9.4, 14.9], and [7.3, 11.3] on the native-workbook panel: a shift of about one point, the same for all 13 sellers. The 55,783-versus-46,867 row discrepancy still has to be reconciled before a number enters the manuscript, but it is immaterial for the conclusion.

Two robustness results carried over from the branch and recomputed on the native panel.

- Cost functional form. With listing-level costs left free (only c at or above zero), the level-deviation restrictions bound the marginal-response-weighted Lerner index from above at 16.5 percent for GadgetPickup, 15.9 for AMS, 16.5 for Upgrade, and 15.8 for We Buy Mobile, with the branch's approximate sampling allowance. The upper bound needs no cap and no cost model: it says only that cutting every price would have gained revenue that costs must eat up. The lower bound needs the no-sale-below-cost cap (8.6, 8.7, 9.6, 9.8 percent with it; 3.5, 6.1, 7.6, 3.9 without). Under flexible demand the upper bounds fall to 12.3 to 15.0 percent, and without the cap the lower bounds become negative. This is the most assumption-light statement in the application: margins on the price-responsive part of these dealers' inventories are at most about 16 percent under the fitted logit demand.
- The sold-unit mean cost is not bounded by level deviations with free costs (the matching principle of the revival note holds on this panel too); Assumption A1 carries the result to the sold-unit average, and the four sellers' sets under A1 lie inside the free-cost intervals.

## 3. The regret notion: the paper's main notion holds on a level action

The branch used Phi-regret against proportional level shifts and claimed it is weaker than the paper's external regret; the memo (M1) correctly says it is neither weaker nor stronger. Today's computation replaces the claim with a comparison. Define the seller's daily level as the geometric mean of its prices over a denominator, and the fixed-level comparator as holding that level at l' every day (the day's prices rescaled by l' over l_d). This is the paper's contextual external regret with the level as the action and relative prices, inventory, and rivals as the unrestricted state. Results at fitted demand and under max-t, GadgetPickup, baseline demand:

| Level measured against | Fitted set | Max-t set | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decluttr quote (the cost reference) | empty | empty | a fixed level relative to Decluttr beats actual play (t = 4.2 at the Phi midpoint) |
| Internal Swappa market index (smoothed rival median) | [11.9, 13.5] | [9.4, 16.8] | not rejected; contains the Phi set |
| Lowest rival price | [10.3, 15.2] | [8.3, 17.9] | not rejected; contains the Phi set |

Relative to the Decluttr quote the fixed-level comparator is rejected at fitted demand for 10 of 13 sellers, including all four large ones. Relative to the Swappa market index it is not rejected at fitted demand for 8 of 13, including GadgetPickup, AMS, Upgrade, and NYCPhonebuyer, and all but one seller have nonempty max-t sets; relative to the rival minimum all 13 do. The economic reading is clean: these dealers set a stable markup over the Swappa market, not over the external quote; the Decluttr value enters as a cost shifter, which is what Assumption A1 says, and not as a pricing anchor. For the paper this answers Q18 in favor of option (b): the application can use the main text's external regret on a level action measured against the market index, with Phi-regret over level shifts as the weaker, tighter robustness, and no nesting claim is needed. The fixed-level sets are wider than the Phi sets because rescaling by a day-specific factor adds variance; they are the honest price of the main-text notion.

## 4. The weak link: the demand response, and what today's checks say about it

Everything above conditions on the first-stage logit. Across the fitted specifications the GadgetPickup Bonferroni set moves from [7.3, 11.3] (flexible price-gap shape) through [9.2, 14.3] (richer rivals plus SKU-week), [9.4, 14.9] (richer rivals), [10.0, 15.4] (baseline), to [10.5, 16.5] (richer rivals plus freshness controls); the branch's 25 percent attenuation gives [14.3, 21.1]. Two new pieces of evidence bear on the direction.

Batch-markdown event study (169 seller-days on which a seller cut at least ten and at least a quarter of its continuing listings, median cut at least one percent; design D4 of the demand note, at the daily stock-hazard level). The seller's hazard in the repriced SKUs rises from about 3 percent before the event to 7 percent on the event day, decaying to 5.4 percent after a week; rivals in the same SKUs are flat at 4.5 percent. The difference-in-differences in log hazards is 0.86 (bootstrap s.e. 0.06) over days 0 to 2 and 0.59 over days 3 to 6. But the per-event regression of that difference on the event's own cut size has an intercept of 0.82 (s.e. 0.09) and a slope of minus 1.5 (s.e. 1.4) over days 0 to 2, minus 2.5 (s.e. 1.3) over days 3 to 6: cuts below 2.5 percent produce almost the same jump as cuts above 5 percent. Two facts show why the events cannot validate the elasticity. The repriced SKUs were underperforming before the cut (hazard falling from 3.4 to 2.6 percent against rivals' 4.5), so timing is endogenous and the pre-period is depressed; and the seller's other SKUs' hazard halves on the event day (10 to 6 percent), so something happens to the seller's whole portfolio on repricing days. Repricing on Swappa carries a non-price effect (visibility, notifications, or whatever else the seller does that day), and deep cuts go to the worst inventory, which biases the cut-size slope toward zero. The slope is therefore neither a validation nor a refutation of the logit's response (about minus 8 in the same units); it is uninformative.

Freshness control. The event finding suggests an omitted variable: if repricing days have higher demand and lower prices, the logit attributes part of the repricing effect to price. Adding changed-today, cut-today, an observation-gap indicator, log days since the last observed price change, and an indicator for two days or fewer since the change to the richer-rivals specification gives a cut-day effect of plus 0.33 log-odds and a hazard that falls with days since the change; the holdout log loss improves (0.16703 against 0.16785); the response of the focal sellers to a uniform 5 percent cut falls by about 10 percent (40.2 to 36.0 percent more sales for GadgetPickup, 44.5 to 39.5 for We Buy Mobile), in the same direction and of the same size as the branch's matched previous-quote check. The Lerner sets move up by about one point. This is a correction worth adopting in the main specification; it does not change the conclusion.

What this leaves. The own-price response is a maintained first stage, as in any demand-based markup estimate, and the data at hand do not offer a design that isolates it: the only within-seller price variation comes with the repricing event itself. The right presentation is the Lerner index as a function of the response, with the demand family (baseline to flexible shape) and the 25 percent attenuation as the stated band, and the behavioral layer (no conduct assumption, a coarse revealed-preference restriction, set identification) as the contribution. A causal design would need a second window with a platform-wide shock; none exists in this scrape, and the one fee change Swappa made (from buyer-only fees to 3 percent on each side) predates the sample.

## 5. What to demote or drop

- The fee counterfactual's "buyers bear most of the fee" claim: pass-through is a statement about demand curvature; the flexible shape doubles the fitted fee response (memo M7). Present the post-fee level shift as a range across the demand family, with profit losses, and rerun the matched-combination joint test only after the main demand specification is fixed. Not recomputed today.
- The recommending-platform counterfactual: keep as the economic reading of the regret notion (a factor of about four between external and swap regret), labeled a finite sensitivity at three cost points (M8).
- The mature-learner allowances (M9), the pathwise envelope T2 (vacuous; keep one sentence as the reason Assumption A2 is needed), and the "weaker than external regret" sentence (M1).
- Nominal 95 percent statements for sellers with fewer than about 550 sales unless the bootstrap set is reported.
- The two-seller selection by the post-hoc rule: report the four largest sellers, or the simultaneous-over-13 sets, and keep the rule as a transparency device.

## 6. How to make the core even stronger, in order

1. Inference of record: the percentile-t moving-block bootstrap with demand refit (today's Unity run), reported next to the Bonferroni test; it closes M3 and exposes where the normal approximation fails. Extend it to the G1 grid and to the fixed-level comparator (cheap; the same job script).
2. Regret notion: make the fixed-level comparator against the Swappa market index the main specification and the level-shift Phi comparator the robustness. Ask Niccolò whether the level action with relative prices in the unrestricted state is acceptable as an instance of Definition `regdyn2` (Q18, option b); the data say it is the one that fits.
3. Demand: adopt the richer-rivals plus freshness specification as the main first stage, report the five-specification band and the 25 percent attenuation, and state conditional price exogeneity as the maintained assumption. Do not add further demand specifications unless they answer a named concern (the memo's rule).
4. Lead with the model-free statement: with no cost model, margins on price-responsive inventory are at most about 16 percent under fitted demand; Assumption A1 adds the lower bound and the sold-unit translation.
5. Reconcile the reference-row discrepancy by a staged count comparison (M5) and record the result; it is a provenance task, not a scientific risk.
6. Report the freshness and event-study facts in one paragraph as the reason the elasticity is a maintained input, and as a small substantive finding about the platform (repricing itself sells).
7. After 2 and 3 are fixed, rerun the fee counterfactual with the joint test across the demand family and present pass-through as a range.
